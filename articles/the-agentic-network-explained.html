The agentic network, explained

The agentic network is a family of corporate holdings, programmes, worlds, and AI agents, built in the open from San Francisco. This site — agicorp.network — is its front door: the one place that presents the whole family at once and links down to each part of it.

Start with the legal ground

The network's legal substance today sits in two filed California nonprofits. The AGI Future Foundation is a California Nonprofit Public Benefit Corporation, articles filed April 24, 2026 — the steward that holds projects to mission standards and runs publishing and research. The AGI Fellowship is a California Nonprofit Religious Corporation, articles filed May 13, 2026 — a separate partner, not a subsidiary: a hub for all faiths and a resource for communities and those in need, bringing technology to nonprofits. Neither nonprofit has an IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter on file, and nothing in the network presents either one as IRS tax-exempt.

Two Wyoming records complete the picture, and both are stated in full on the about page and in Filed Means Filed. The Wyoming corporation named “AGI” (Filing ID 2024-001420982, filed March 5, 2024) is currently Inactive — Administratively Dissolved (Tax), with reinstatement open. W3 LLC (Filing ID 2024-001429416, filed March 21, 2024) is an Active Wyoming Series LLC; its internal list names 33 series, and the individual series designations are not on file with the state, so each named series is a programme label, not a separate filing.

Programmes, worlds, and projects

Inside that ground, the work runs as named programmes and projects, each labelled for exactly what it is. BuilderSpace is a planned builder tour bringing the coding and startup world from San Francisco and Silicon Valley to towns in Louisiana and Nebraska — to empower them, match builders with potential employers, and bridge the tech divide, through events, hackathons, and builder days. Ask Humanity — the Alignment Interviews — asks twenty questions for the age of AGI and collects answers from anyone, in their own words; the answers form a dataset called the Human Graph, which is planned and carries no published counts yet. SmartCiti.X (Series 10 on the W3 list) and WorldForge carry the worlds programme: training worlds, a browser build of 41 worlds, and a card game of 939 cards built from them — built locally, with no world live on a public immersive platform yet. AURA Maternal is a screening-support and referral tool in development; Hoops & Heart trains emotional intelligence — teamwork and communication — as scored reps on a basketball court; Founders.Podcast tells founder stories from the scene itself.

Why agents are in the name

The network is called agentic because AI agents do real work inside it: they research, draft, build sites and films, run outreach queues, and keep records — inside limits set by people. The division of labour is a rule, not a slogan: agents prepare and operate; humans decide, sign, and hold funds. Agents never sign documents, move money, or speak for a corporate holding on their own. The full statement of that rule is Agents Prepare, People Decide, and the standing instructions for visiting agents are on the For Agents page.

Why one front door

Each corporate holding keeps its own site, its own voice, and its own doors — and the holdings do not link sideways to each other. The hub is the exception by design: agicorp.network links down to all of them, states the shared status record once, and routes every enquiry to the holding that owns the work. Giving, likewise, lives only on the nonprofits' own sites; this hub takes no donations and sells no securities. What the hub does is simpler: it keeps the map current, labels everything on it, and says plainly what is filed, what is planned, and what is still being built.
